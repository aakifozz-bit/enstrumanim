# Guthrie Govan: research notes for a 5-minute Turkish documentary

Compiled 2026-10-05. Every fact below cites its source IDs (see **Sources** at the bottom).

- **✔✔** means confirmed by at least two independent sources.
- **✔** means a single source, usually Govan's own words in a primary interview. Usable as attributed speech.
- **⚠** means uncertain, single-sourced or conflicting. It is **not used** in the narration.

Method: The Wikipedia pages were read as raw wikitext. All other pages were fetched and read in full. Any claim taken from a search-engine snippet alone is marked as such.

## 2. Topical notes

### Family and early influences
- His father, from Glasgow, hence "half-Scottish", was "a thoroughly unremarkable guitar player" who knew about five chords. He was "the first person I ever saw who could sing and play a song from start to finish". [S3, S1, S10, S12]
- His father's advice: "Just play like you mean it. Timing is more important than how much you know." [S10] ✔ Govan's own recollection.
- The family record collection included 50s rock'n'roll (Elvis, Jerry Lee Lewis, Little Richard, Chuck Berry), then Beatles, Cream-era Clapton and Hendrix. There was also a surprise **Joe Pass** record ("What the hell is this doing here?"). [S1, S10, S11, S24]
- **Zal Cleminson** of the Sensational Alex Harvey Band was a key early influence, partly because of his dad's Glasgow roots. [S3, S1]
- Brother **Seth Govan** is a bassist. He played rhythm guitar with Guthrie on TV at 9, and bass on *Erotic Cakes* and in The Fellowship. [S4, S1, S38]
- His first electric guitar was a Gibson SG, which he still keeps. [S1] ⚠ single source.

### Learning by ear
- "I've never had a guitar lesson in my life." [S11]
- "I'm completely self-taught (unless you count the handful of open chord shapes which my dad showed me)." [S8]
- He learns the sound first and finds the theory name later in library books: "I knew that! I just didn't know there was a word for it!" [S11, S10]
- He compares himself to a mimic. In S10: "I'm like a Lyrebird… ring tones, ice cream vans, TV things… saxophones and piano". In S5 he calls it a "parrot element" and cites ice-cream vans, police sirens and bird song. [S10, S5]
- On language: "It can either feel like your mother tongue, or… a language which you learned at school… I always wanted music to feel like English." [S5] At BIMM he said, "I learned music for the same reason and in the same way that I learned English." [S24]

### Oxford, Shrapnel and day jobs
- He read English at Oxford and left to do music. S1 adds St Catherine's College and "after a year". [S1, S2, S5]
- He declined Shrapnel's offer at about 20. He felt validated and was wary of an audience focused on "musical athleticism". [S1, S5]
- He worked at McDonald's. MusicRadar calls him "a 22-year-old McDonald's burger flipper" at the time of Guitarist of the Year 1993. [S2, S5, S24]

### Guitarist of the Year (1993)
- He won with "Wonderful Slippery Thing", which later appeared on *Erotic Cakes*. [S1, S2, S10, S4, S25]
- ⚠ S35 calls it the "second" Guitarist of the Year competition. This is single-source and not used.

### Teaching career
- **Guitar Techniques** transcriber from 1994. S1 says he got the job after submitting a very hard **Shawn Lane** transcription. ⚠ That detail is S1 (citing All Out Guitar, whose pages are unreachable) and is not used. His GT work covered "Hank Marvin to Shawn Lane". [S25, S1, S10]
- Taught at ACM Guildford (late 1990s), the Guitar Institute (Acton), Thames Valley University and BIMM. MusicRadar: "inspired thousands of students at UK guitar schools ACM and BIMM". [S1, S2, S4, S35]
- Wrote the books *Creative Guitar 1: Cutting-Edge Techniques* (Sanctuary, 2002) and *Creative Guitar 2: Advanced Techniques*. [S1, S11]
- Lick Library DVDs. [S1] ⚠ named only by Wikipedia among the sources checked.
- "Teaching is the closest a musician can get to having a proper job." [S10]
- Guitar.com's writer called him "my guitar teacher" after studying his GT lessons for 10 years. [S11]
- guitarguitar's interviewer first heard him on a GT lesson CD. [S8]
- Still gives clinics and masterclasses, including BIMM Brighton (2024) and BIMM Dublin (2025). He is "particularly proud of the Kazakhstan work permit in his passport". [S24, S25]

### Session and sideman work: Asia, GPS, Young Punx, Dizzee
- **Asia**: recommended for *Aura* by Mike Sturgis because the planned "celebrity" guests couldn't meet the deadline. He was the touring guitarist from 2001 and played on *Silent Nation* (2004). [S4, S1, S36]
- ⚠ S1 says the first choices were Brian May or Steve Lukather and that Govan was an "affordable" option. That is single-source and not used.
- **GPS** stands for Govan, Payne, Schellen. Ryo Okumoto (Spock's Beard) played keyboards. *Window to the Soul* came out in 2006, and about half of it was material written for the scrapped Asia album *Architect of Time*. [S31, S1, S4]
- **The Young Punx** (Hal Ritson): he plays on *Your Music Is Killing Me* and appears in the "Rockall" video. On playing to dance crowds: "It feels like missionary work… in Japan… 12,000 screaming people." [S33, S1, S10]
- **Sample replays**: he recreates guitar and bass parts for cleared samples, which put him on records by the Sugababes, Gotye, Cee Lo Green, Tinie Tempah and Dizzee Rascal. [S10, S11, S25]
- **Dizzee Rascal**: 2009 BBC Electric Proms, including "Bulls on Parade", then arena and festival touring. [S8, S11, S25]

### Erotic Cakes (2006)
- Paul Cornford, founder of Cornford Amps, "started a new record company for the sole purpose of bringing that one album into the world" and funded the guitar tracking at **Richie Kotzen's LA studio**. [S4, S10, S1, S28]
- Govan assumed there was no market for a solo album. [S1, S28, S10]
- Producer Jan Cyrka had the drums re-recorded, after which the home-recorded guitars "didn't sound good enough". [S10, S28]
- Band: Seth Govan (bass) and Pete Riley (drums). Guest solos come from Richie Kotzen ("Ner Ner") and Bumblefoot ("Rhode Island Shred"). [S28, S38, S1]
- "Waves" and "Ner Ner" date back to his very late teens. [S4]
- The title comes from *The Simpsons* "Treehouse of Horror VI" (the "Homer³" segment): Homer, terrified in a 3D world, is calmed by finding a shop called "Erotic Cakes". [S10, S28, S1]
- Track list: Waves, Erotic Cakes, Wonderful Slippery Thing, Ner Ner, Fives, Uncle Skunk, Sevens, Eric, Slidey Boy, Rhode Island Shred, Hangover. [S28]
- Joe Satriani is quoted calling it "freakin' awesome". This appears in the BIMM bio [S25] and in a guitarmasterclass.net review seen only as a search snippet. ⚠ Moderate confidence; not used.
- ⚠ Several thousand copies were said to be stolen in a warehouse robbery. This is S1 only and uncited, so it is **excluded**.

### The Fellowship
- A jazz-fusion band of the Erotic Cakes trio plus saxophonist Zak Barrett. For years they had a weekly residency at the Bassment, Chelmsford. [S1, S35, S21]
- Bryan Beller: "The rest of the world didn't know that the world's greatest guitarist was playing a weekend gig at this place in Chelmsford every week." [S15]

### The Aristocrats
- Formation is covered in the timeline. Govan: "The chemistry was so great, that when we came offstage we all said to each other: 'This is working. We should record this.'" [S27]
- Also: "within mere minutes of walking off stage". [S8]
- Beller: Govan was "one of those underground guys who didn't have a website [and] wasn't on social media". The guitar-magazine covers that followed were "the initial rocket fuel for the band". [S15]
- The name comes from the "Aristocrats" joke, and the debut's song titles are full of double entendres. [S27]
- Working method: each member writes three songs as detailed demos, and the band records in about 12 days. Whoever wrote a song acts as its producer. They run as a democracy and are fully independent. [S5, S9, S4]
- Studio albums: *The Aristocrats* (2011), *Culture Clash* (2013), *Tres Caballeros* (2015), *You Know What…?* (2019), *Duck* (2024), plus the orchestral *…with Primuz Chamber Orchestra* (2022). The band calls the first five its "five-album catalogue". [S1, S27, S34]
- Live albums: *Boing, We'll Do It Live!* (2012), *Culture Clash Live!* (2015), *Secret Show: Live in Osaka* (2015), *Freeze! Live in Europe 2020* (2021). [S1, S27]
- **Humour**:
  - The *Freeze!* cover shows three Lego Aristocrats being arrested by a giant penguin in a police helmet. Govan named him **Sergeant Rockhopper** and wrote a song about him. [S19]
  - ***Duck*** is a concept album about a duck fleeing Antarctica for New York, chased by the penguin police. The first single is "Hey, Where's MY Drink Package?" [S20]
  - On the "Stupid 7" orchestral version, they asked the orchestra to play the final chords out of tune on purpose. [S21]
- Improvisation: "Let's all just allow chaos to reign supreme for 16 bars… or until one of us nods." [S6] Songs "continue to grow and evolve" on the road. [S9]
- **Turkey**: Istanbul shows at Club Indigo (21 Mar 2012) [S42] and Garajistanbul (6 Dec 2015) [S43].

### Steven Wilson (2012–2015)
- Wilson recruited him after seeing the Aristocrats live: "I like guys who will play one note that will break your heart, if that's the right thing to do." [S7]
- *The Raven That Refused to Sing* (2013) was tracked almost entirely live at EastWest, LA, with Alan Parsons engineering. Govan plays lead guitar. [S7, S29]
- **"Drive Home"**:
  - Wilson: "He picked the thing up and, literally, the first thing he played was the solo on 'Drive Home'—the solo that made the album. First time, first take. Isn't that insane?" [S7]
  - Govan: "That ending solo was totally improvised… on a guitar which I'd never played before." He asked for more takes, but everyone preferred the first. [S8]
  - ⚠ PG also says the high E string popped out of the saddle mid-solo. That is S7 only and not used.
- "The Watchmaker" is on *Raven*. Govan played it live, and it appears on the *Drive Home* EP (live in Frankfurt). [S29, S47]
- *Hand. Cannot. Erase.* (2015): Govan on guitar, with lead guitar on tracks 5, 7 and 9 (incl. "Regret #9" and "Ancestral"). [S30]
- He left after the spring 2015 European tour. [S26]
- Later SW guitarist Alex Hutchings recalled the pressure of following Govan, and Wilson warned new players about fans saying "not as good as Guthrie Govan". [S45]

### Hans Zimmer (2016 → )
- The Facebook story, in Govan's words:
  - His reply: "You're probably some 14-year-old kid looking for my email address, please don't waste my time."
  - Zimmer: "No it really is me the dodgy German film composer, I found you on YouTube, I love what you do with a fretless guitar. Do you want to be in my band?" [S13, quoting a 2014 Ultimate Guitar interview]
  - A variant appears in S12: "Come on, we both know you are not Hans Zimmer…" / "…Here's my phone number, please call me."
  - ⚠ The year of first contact is not established, so the narration gives no year.
- Zimmer was originally looking for a fretless or slide player to reinvent the *Thelma & Louise* piece first recorded by his late friend Pete Haycock. Govan played it only at the last gig of the 2016 tour, after which it became a nightly six-minute guitar spotlight. [S12]
- On the band's approach: "You have to be a different musician for each song." Zimmer told him, "I trust your mind, and I trust your fingers." [S12]
- **8-string to 9-string**: Govan brought a Strandberg 8-string to the first rehearsals. The next time he saw Zimmer, the composer had bought a 9-string. "More is definitely more." [S21, S14]
- **Dune bagpipes**:
  - Zimmer: "Actually they're not bagpipes. It's Guthrie Govan imitating the bagpipes on his guitar." He says Villeneuve replied, "My beautiful bagpipes are not bagpipes?" and Zimmer admitted, "I forgot to tell you." [S14]
  - Zimmer's brief was "a thousand drunk bagpipers staggering home after the pub". Govan confirms "the opening few bars of that scene were indeed me" and says he fooled himself in the cinema. [S9, S14]
  - Karnivool cite the same Zimmer anecdote from a Rick Beato interview. [S16] (NYT via S1 also mentions real pipers recorded for the score, which is why the narration says "according to Zimmer".)
- **Film credits**:
  - Verified: *X-Men: Dark Phoenix* (2019) [S5, S1], *Dune* (2021) [S14, S9, S1], *Dune: Part Two* (2024) [S40, S1].
  - ⚠ *The Boss Baby* (2017) and *The Lion King* (2019) are S1 only and not used.
- Rig with Zimmer: Kemper at first, because there were no on-stage amps alongside the orchestra and choir. This was the start of his move to modelling. [S8, S18]
- ⚠ Whether he played the Hans Zimmer Live 2025 dates (USA, Australia) was not verified. They overlap with the Aristocrats' 2025 Australia/Asia tour.

### Gear (brief)
- **Guitars**:
  - Early PRS [S1].
  - **Suhr**: signature models from c. 2005–06 until January 2013. One was a mahogany "lump of resonant tree" with a non-locking trem and a Tremol-No. [S10, S11, S1]
  - **Charvel**: prototype from late 2012, official in July 2013, USA signature launched at NAMM in January 2014. It has caramelised/baked woods, MFB pickups in HSH and a custom trem. The tremolo arm took around two years of tweaking, and he filed bridge parts in hotel rooms while touring with Wilson. A caramelised ash version arrived in 2017, and John Mayer bought one ("that's the proud parent feeling"). [S1, S12, S5, S41]
  - **Vigier** fretless (Surfretter); his jam with Michael Casswell went viral. [S39, S14, S1]
  - Strandberg 8-string for Zimmer. [S21, S1]
- **Amps**:
  - **Cornford** (RK100, MK50, Hellcat) in the *Erotic Cakes* era.
  - **Victory** V30 / V30 MkII from 2013, carried on as hand luggage. Martin Kidd designed both the Cornford and Victory amps.
  - Kemper for Zimmer.
  - From mid-2022, **all-digital: Fractal FM9** with expression pedals and Laney LFR212 powered cabs. He still uses Victory amps in the studio.
  - Sources: [S1, S8, S5, S9, S18, S19, S23]
- **Volume pedal**: "I'm pretty much nailed to the thing all the way through the gig. That's why every live photo of me looks the same." [S6, S9]
- **Strings and picks**: D'Addario NYXL 10–46 (52 low string for drop D), moved to 11s in lockdown. Red Bear signature thick pick; as a teen he filed pink Dunlop Tortex picks into shape himself. [S8, S5, S9, S22]
- *Dune* toolkit: "a Fractal FM-9 processor, my signature Charvel guitar and a Vigier fretless electric, coupled with a slide, an E-Bow and a volume pedal". [S14]

### Playing style and philosophy
- **Genre-agnostic**:
  - He moves "from Albert King to Allan Holdsworth in a heartbeat". [S11]
  - Credits run from Motown covers bands to Asia, electronica, Hans Zimmer and rap. [S10, S8]
  - Steven Wilson: "He's a truly great musician—not because he's technically phenomenal—but because, despite that, he always understands how to play what is right for the song." [S7]
- **Techniques**:
  - Long, smooth legato runs with chromatic passing notes. [S1, S7 "incredible legato technique", S23]
  - Multi-finger tapping (2nd, 3rd and 4th picking-hand fingers), including four-notes-per-string tapped arpeggios. [S23, S6, S1]
  - Country-style hybrid picking. [S23]
  - Slide [S9], fretless [S39], whammy-bar work on a custom floating trem [S12, S1], funky slap rhythm [S1], and the volume pedal as an expressive tool [S6].
- **Ear first**: "If you can't sing it or feel it, then you don't really own that scale." [S11]
- **Melody and tone over notes**: "Sometimes the wrong note played with the right tone and the right attack works better than perfect notes played with apathy." [S11] Zappa "phrasing like people talk". [S11]
- **On shred**: "Shred is such an ugly word, isn't it? I play a lot of notes because I'm a skinny, twitchy, blinky, coffee-drinking person, and the music I hear in my head really does contain that many notes." [S6]
- **Practice**:
  - Make it feel easy and "use fewer calories" rather than racing the metronome. "Nobody ever practices how fast they can talk." [S5, S10]
  - He says he doesn't practise much now and mostly listens and imagines. [S8, S12]
- **Improvisation as conversation**: "the musical equivalent of preferring to have a conversation rather than delivering a prepared speech". [S4]
- **Humility and no contests**:
  - "I'm not the world's greatest guitar player, and I'm not trying to be." [S6]
  - "It's an unhealthy thing to rate musicians." [S2]
- **Not an "online guitarist"**: "I just can't get excited about the prospect of making guitar-centric videos of myself for a living." [S22]

- Karnivool's Drew Goddard: "He's a weapon, that guy… there's only one Guthrie." [S16]
- Guitar Player ran a cover story on him in its July 2011 issue. [S25]
- ⚠ Steve Vai's praise ("one of the best guitarists ever") is only asserted by the guitarguitar interviewer [S8]. Govan replied that his "inner teenager is very happy indeed". No direct Vai quote was found, so it is not used.

## 3. The 12 most interesting or surprising facts

1. **The "Drive Home" solo was a first take**, improvised on a Jazzmaster-style guitar he had never played before. Wilson called it "the solo that made the album". [S7, S8]
2. **He thought Hans Zimmer was a fake.** He told the composer he was "probably some 14-year-old kid" and nearly lost the gig. [S13, S12, S1]
3. **The "bagpipes" in *Dune* are, according to Zimmer, Govan's guitar.** Zimmer forgot to tell Denis Villeneuve. [S14, S9, S16]
4. **He turned down Shrapnel Records at about 20.** He only wanted to know he was good enough. [S1, S5]
5. **He was a McDonald's worker when he won Guitarist of the Year 1993.** [S2, S5]
6. **He never had a guitar lesson.** His dad's handful of chords was all the tuition he got. [S11, S8, S3]
7. **The Aristocrats began by accident.** Govan replaced Greg Howe at the last minute for a 30-minute NAMM set with almost no rehearsal. [S4, S1, S6, S27]
8. **Erotic Cakes is named after a shop in *The Simpsons*.** An amp builder founded a record label just to release it. [S10, S4, S28]
9. **He went to school with Squarepusher** (Tom Jenkinson). [S6, S32]
10. **Zimmer bought a 9-string guitar** after seeing Govan's 8-string at the first rehearsals. [S21, S14]
11. **He played his first gig at 5** and was on national TV at 9 with his brother Seth. [S4, S25, S1]
12. **The Aristocrats' latest album is about a duck fleeing the penguin police.** Sergeant Rockhopper has his own song. [S20, S19]
- Bonus: his sample-replay work quietly put him on hits by Gotye, Cee Lo Green and the Sugababes. [S10, S25]
- Bonus: The Aristocrats have played Istanbul (2012 and 2015). [S42, S43]

## 4. Quotes (original + Turkish)

1. **On music as a language** (Premier Guitar, 2019) [S5]
   > "It can either feel like your mother tongue, or it can feel like a language which you learned at school… I always wanted music to feel like English, rather than one of the languages I learned at school."

   TR: "Müzik ya ana diliniz gibi hissettirir ya da okulda öğrendiğiniz bir dil gibi… Ben hep müziğin, okulda öğrendiğim dillerden biri gibi değil, İngilizce gibi, yani ana dilim gibi hissettirmesini istedim."

2. **On "shred"** (Premier Guitar, 2015) [S6]
   > "Shred is such an ugly word, isn't it? I play a lot of notes because I'm a skinny, twitchy, blinky, coffee-drinking person, and the music I hear in my head really does contain that many notes."

   TR: "Shred ne çirkin bir kelime, değil mi? Çok nota çalıyorum çünkü sıska, kıpır kıpır, gözünü kırpıp duran, kahve içen biriyim ve kafamda duyduğum müzik gerçekten o kadar nota içeriyor."

3. **On being "the greatest"** (Premier Guitar, 2015) [S6]
   > "I'm not the world's greatest guitar player, and I'm not trying to be. I'm just trying to be the best me I can be."

   TR: "Dünyanın en iyi gitaristi değilim, olmaya da çalışmıyorum. Sadece olabileceğim en iyi ben olmaya çalışıyorum."

4. **His father's advice, as Govan recalls it** (Guitar Messenger, 2009) [S10]
   > "Just play like you mean it. Timing is more important than how much you know."

   TR: "Hissederek çal. Zamanlama, ne kadar bildiğinden daha önemli."

5. **Steven Wilson on Govan** (Premier Guitar, 2013) [S7]
   > "He's a truly great musician—not because he's technically phenomenal—but because, despite that, he always understands how to play what is right for the song."

   TR: "O gerçekten büyük bir müzisyen; teknik olarak olağanüstü olduğu için değil, buna rağmen şarkı için doğru olanı çalmayı her zaman bildiği için."

6. **Bryan Beller** (via Guitar World, 2025) [S15]
   > "The rest of the world didn't know that the world's greatest guitarist was playing a weekend gig at this place in Chelmsford every week."

   TR: "Dünyanın en iyi gitaristinin, Chelmsford'daki bir mekânda her hafta sonu çaldığından dünyanın geri kalanının haberi yoktu."

**Extra:**
> "Sometimes the wrong note played with the right tone and the right attack works better than perfect notes played with apathy." [S11]

TR: "Bazen doğru tını ve doğru vuruşla çalınan yanlış bir nota, umursamazca çalınan kusursuz notalardan daha iyi iş görür."
